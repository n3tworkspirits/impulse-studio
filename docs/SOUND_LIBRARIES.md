# Sample library choices

The original curated bank contains 232 stereo samples. Additional complete loops and phrases are appended in the manifest. They are local WAV files: playback needs no internet connection, account, plugin, or paid sampler. The original 232 samples use CC0; appended loops include CC0, CC BY 3.0 and CC BY 4.0 sources, as recorded in `src/recordings/licenses/loop-credits.json`. Required third-party credits must be retained; copies of the source licences ship in `src/recordings/licenses`. The manifest records exact sources, pinned revisions and checksums. Existing sound IDs remain reserved; the original 59 recordings retain IDs 232–290 and later samples are appended without moving existing entries.

These are practical choices for this app's free, redistributable library. Quality is instrument-specific; this is not a claim that one library is universally the best. The starter bank uses one recorded pitch/articulation per entry, rather than full velocity layers and round robins. Pitching far from its sampled range changes the tone and duration. Further multisampling is the next improvement for realism across the keyboard.

The psytrance sources are useful building blocks, not a complete specialist psytrance production pack. Keep the tracker's existing designed psy kicks, acid plucks, squelches, zaps and risers alongside them. The existing synthetic presets cover gaps such as lute, dulcimer and accordion. A full trained opera singer library remains a gap; a name alone should not imply that a real recording of that instrument is installed.

The choir samples contain performances and harmonies, not eight clean single-pitch vowels. They play once at their original speed when assigned to a pad. They are appropriate for atmospheric choir phrases and dungeon synth textures, rather than replacing a chromatically multisampled solo vocalist. Use the recorded growls for metal, and the newer sung-vowel entries cover real solo/crowd voices, while these choir phrases remain separate performances.

## Expanded collections

The next 47 recordings append to the existing catalogue without changing its first 185 recordings or their IDs. They add 15 instruments, 11 percussion recordings, 17 Ork vocal shouts/growl chops, and four vocal phrases/textures. Collections are **Keys & mallets**, **Acoustic instruments**, **Acoustic percussion**, **Vocal shouts & chops**, and **Vocal phrases & textures**. VCSL supplies the new instruments and percussion; Karoryfer 272 Merry Orks supplies the vocal effects. Both ship under their existing CC0 licences.

Additional CC0 vocal sources are [owstu’s long female vocal](https://freesound.org/people/owstu/sounds/628817/), [mahakush’s male hum](https://freesound.org/people/mahakush/sounds/368817/), [jdagenet’s male vocal drone](https://freesound.org/people/jdagenet/sounds/395431/), and [buggly’s improvised female melody](https://freesound.org/people/buggly/sounds/700247/). The bank uses public HQ MP3 previews, retaining up to the first eight seconds as playable phrases. These play once at source speed; they do not stretch automatically to song tempo.

New keyboard/mallet and acoustic entries are available as F4 instrument presets, alongside vocal shout/growl kits and an acoustic percussion/roll kit. The arranger supports collection filtering and searches sound names, collection names, recording types and source libraries. Previewing a built-in sound does not allocate song slots. Sung vowels start in their recorded register; percussion and vocals receive the appropriate layer group.

## Vocal playback

Sung vowels are pitched recordings with sustain loops; selecting or resetting one starts at its recorded register so low male voices are not previewed as unnaturally high sounds. Pitching far away still changes formants and duration. These small CC0 sets are not full professional soprano/tenor multisample libraries. Female sung voice chooses the nearest of five source notes (A3–E4); spoken chops play once at reference speed.

Freesound entries use its publicly available HQ MP3 previews. They are lossy sources even though converted to local WAV for playback. Each source page explicitly lists CC0; the bank retains exact source URLs and checksums. Downloaded original WAVs could replace these previews in a future quality upgrade.

## Drum presets

Eleven ready-made drum kits can be loaded from the Pads screen onto pads 1–12, retaining other pads and existing patterns. They are also note-mapped instrument presets in F4: House, Deep house, Techno, Garage, Electro, Psytrance, Latin, World, Acoustic metal, and Dance percussion & FX. The psytrance kit combines the existing designed psy kick/clap/zap/riser with new sampled drums and percussion.

## Browsing and use

In Pads, choose **Assign → Collection** and select the relevant existing collection (Orchestral, Dungeon synth, Classic, Metal, Vocals & opera, Psytrance or World percussion). Preview plays recordings at the reference note, and gives live choir phrases time to finish. Selecting a drum, vocal effect or choir phrase sets the pad to original/reference speed and single-note mode. Pitched instruments retain the pad's musical note. Existing assignments and song PCM are not rewritten.

The Instruments library also includes recorded sustained instruments, ringing instruments, a recorded metal drum kit, a live choir phrase kit and a metal vocal kit. WAV recordings, tuning and sustain loops survive IT export. Unedited recorded entries are built-in sounds and therefore support compressed song links; edited/imported recordings still need a file export.

## Licences and rejected options

CC0 permits redistribution of the audio in the app. The exact licences accompany the recordings, including the Signature Sounds licence supplied in the ZIP. “Free” and “royalty-free” alone do not establish redistribution rights. For example, [SampleRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/samples/sampleradar-485-free-tape-treated-samples) permits musical use but explicitly prohibits redistributing its samples, so its packs are not bundled. Sonatina's older Sampling Plus licence and mixed provenance make VSCO/VCSL preferable for this CC0-only starter bank.

Paid Kontakt libraries, DAW-only plugins and packs whose EULAs prohibit raw-sample redistribution are outside this selection. No such libraries have been installed or repackaged.

## Rebuilding

`tools/recording-sources.json` defines the stable catalogue order. Append entries; do not reorder them. Run `python3 tools/import-recordings.py` to import appended entries while retaining verified existing WAVs. For a complete rebuild use `--rebuild-all --choir-zip /path/to/SS_Live_Childrens_Choir_CC0.zip` with numpy and ffmpeg available. The script downloads pinned sources, verifies their CC0 licence, removes unused silence/DC, converts to stereo 32 kHz/16-bit, applies short edge fades and headroom, creates long crossfaded sustain regions for held instruments, and records SHA-256 hashes. Root notes were checked against the audio because source octave naming varies.
